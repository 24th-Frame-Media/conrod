# The two Conrods: research for the brand mark (2026-11-19)

## Conrod Straight, Mount Panorama

- **1.916 km**, the fastest section of the 6.213 km circuit, with V8 Supercars touching ~300 km/h.
  Shane van Gisbergen was timed at **300.5 km/h** in a pre-race session for the 2023 Bathurst 1000;
  the closed-cockpit lap record run (1:56.605) peaked at **308.71 km/h**.
- It is a **descent with two distinct crests**. Before 1987 there was no chicane: the faster cars
  were getting **airborne over the second hump**, which contributed to at least one fatal accident.
- **The Chase** was added in 1987 for the World Touring Car Championship, because the FIA required
  straights under **1,200 m**. It is three turns: a fast right (often called "The Kink"), then a
  120 km/h left, then the run to Murray's Corner. It made the straight slower and created one of the
  fastest corners in the world.
- **The name is literal.** It was "Main Straight" until a **con-rod failure ended Frank Kleinig's
  1939 Easter race** in the Kleinig/Hudson. The corner where it happened took the part's name.
- Every driver who has died at Mount Panorama in a high-speed accident died on that straight,
  with the exception of Denny Hulme, who suffered a heart attack.

Sources: Mount Panorama Circuit (Wikipedia); Project Motor Racing, "Bathurst Comes to Project
Motor Racing"; performancewholesale.com.au circuit notes.

## The part

- Two ends of **different size**: the **small end** (small eye) rides the piston pin on a bronze
  bush; the **big end** (large eye) wraps the crankpin in bearing shells.
- The **big end is split**: a cap held by two high-strength **rod bolts**, or a **fracture-split
  ("cracked") cap** where the cap and rod are one forging broken and re-mated, so the seam is
  nearly invisible and alignment is exact.
- The **shank is tapered and waisted**, and its cross-section is an **I-beam** (or H-beam): the
  flanges carry the bending load, the thin web saves weight. Many performance rods drill
  **lightening holes** through the web. Forged steel; titanium and aluminium in racing.
- Reading the silhouette: two eyes of unequal size + a waisted, tapered shank. Reading it closely:
  the **I-beam slot**, the **cap bolts** flanking the big end, and the oil passage.

Sources: onallcylinders.com "Connecting Rod Basics"; bar-tek.com "All about con-rods";
engineprofessional.com "Connecting Rods"; firgelliauto.com "Connecting Rod End".

## What this means for the mark

1. **The asymmetry is the truth.** Two different ends, never a symmetric dumbbell. C failed
   because it made the eyes equal and it read as a magnifying glass.
2. **The I-beam is the detail that says "engine part".** E's drilled web does this at 32 px and up.
   The cap bolts are the second cue at 64 px and up. Both are accurate to the part.
3. **The straight is the shank.** Calling the shaft the straight is honest: a rod is the straightest
   thing in an engine, and the mark's waist is the circuit's descent.
4. **The Chase is a kink, not a wobble.** If we want the circuit inside the mark, put it in the
   **slot** (the road), not the silhouette: the slot runs straight, then jogs right once, then back.
   Kinking the outline (B) read as a mistake; the slot is where a viewer expects a change of line.
5. **The crests are a scale cue, not a shape cue.** Two humps in a small icon read as damage or as a
   blob (D). Keep them out of the icon; they belong in a wordmark or a loading animation if anywhere.
6. **The name has a story worth one line.** "Named after a con-rod that let go in 1939" is the
   About screen and the website, not the icon.

## Decision so far

E (I-beam, cap bolts, waisted shank) is the mark that survives 16 px and stays truthful. Open
choices: upright or 35 degrees; and whether the slot carries The Chase as one clean jog.
